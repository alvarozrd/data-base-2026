Influence of sample size and randomness on the morphological classification of galaxies: a 
comparison between Random Forest and linear Support Vector Machine. 
ABSTRACT:
 Large-scale galaxy morphological classification depends on both the model and the 
conditions defining the training, validation, and test sets. This study investigates whether sample size 
and the pseudorandom number generator \(PRNG\) modify the performance and stability of Random 
Forest and linear SVM. We used 34,874 galaxies from the COSMOS Zurich Structure and 
Morphology Catalog, represented by seven structural features and three classes. The protocol 
combined two classifiers, four sample fractions, three PRNGs, and 30 seeds, totaling 720 runs and 360 
exact pairs. Random Forest outperformed linear SVM under every condition, and the gap increased 
with sample size: from 2.43 to 3.06 percentage points in accuracy and from 2.69 to 3.95 points in 
macro F1. Sample size significantly changed this gap, whereas no evidence of a PRNG effect was 
found. Furthermore, between-seed dispersion decreased with more data for both models. These 
findings reinforce the need for paired runs and multiple seeds in reproducible evaluations.
